# Research Plan

## Objective

Create a defensible, source-backed model that allows users to explore whether full AI sovereignty is achievable and what forms of partial sovereignty are realistic.

## Research questions

1. What does AI sovereignty mean in operational, legal and supply-chain terms?
2. Which capabilities must remain available for a national AI ecosystem to function?
3. Which capabilities are domestically owned, locally hosted, allied-access or import-dependent?
4. Which global chokepoints have low substitutability and long replacement times?
5. How do announced projects differ from operational capacity?
6. What would fail if a supplier, country, technology or cross-border route became unavailable?
7. Which dependencies can be reduced, and which can only be diversified?

## Research workstreams

### 1. Definitions and analytical framework

Produce precise definitions for:

- Operational sovereignty
- Legal and governance sovereignty
- Supply-chain sovereignty
- Data sovereignty
- Model sovereignty
- Compute sovereignty
- Energy sovereignty
- Strategic autonomy
- Resilience versus independence

Deliverable: a terminology document and a transparent scoring or classification methodology.

### 2. Source audit

For every source:

- Confirm the URL resolves
- Confirm publication date
- Confirm that the source is primary
- Capture the exact supported claim
- Distinguish disclosed fact from company aspiration
- Record whether a commitment is binding, conditional or non-binding
- Mark the next required review date

Deliverable: an audited source ledger.

### 3. Capability completeness

For each ecosystem, review all 13 capability layers and classify each as:

- Domestic
- Mixed ownership
- Foreign-owned local presence
- Allied access
- Import-dependent
- Under development
- Absent
- Research required

Deliverable: a capability completeness matrix with no unexplained empty cells.

### 4. Dependency graph validation

Every relationship must specify:

- Direction
- Dependency category
- Operational status
- Criticality
- Substitutability
- Confidence
- Evidence
- Whether the relationship is directly observed or inferred

Deliverable: a graph with no unlabeled or unsupported edges.

### 5. National deep dives

Initial priority:

1. United States
2. China
3. Taiwan, South Korea and Japan
4. European Union
5. Israel
6. Gulf states
7. India

Each deep dive should include domestic strengths, foreign dependencies, policy levers and plausible failure scenarios.

### 6. Scenario design

Research scenarios such as:

- Loss of access to leading-edge accelerators
- Failure of an HBM supplier
- Export restriction on lithography equipment
- Data-center power shortage
- Loss of a hyperscaler relationship
- Submarine cable disruption
- Restrictions on cross-border data access
- Loss of a model API
- Local operation without software updates or spare parts

Deliverable: scenario definitions that can be executed entirely in the browser.

## Quality gates

A dataset version is eligible for a reviewed release only when:

- Every numeric claim has a source
- Every source has been opened and checked
- Operational and planned capacity are separated
- Private valuation is not confused with market capitalization
- Every national ecosystem includes all capability layers
- Analytical inference is visibly labeled
- A domain reviewer has checked each major ecosystem
- The validation report contains no errors
- Known gaps are publicly documented

## Research outcome

The desired outcome is not a claim that sovereign AI is possible or impossible. The outcome is a transparent evidence base that makes the trade-offs visible.
